1. Executive summary

Winter is harder for electrolytes because the category’s mental model is sweat = permission to take salt. When she’s not sweating, salt feels extra or risky — while heating quietly dries her face and cold mutes thirst.

Skin Salt’s winter job is not “refreshing glow electrolytes.” It is a drinkable winter barrier / skin-hydration ritual for women who already buy creams (and often collagen), whose jars stop working under forced air.

Core thesis. Winter doesn’t give sweat-permission for salt — heating drains skin and cold mutes thirst. Skin Salt is the drinkable winter skin-support stick (HA + ceramides + Vitamin C + DermaVal) with 500 mg sodium (half of LMNT’s typical 1,000 mg) so water works when cream alone can’t.

Hero hierarchy (winter)

  1. Skin matrix (HA + ceramides + DermaVal) — why winter
  2. Electrolytes as delivery / cellular hydration — how water sticks
  3. Energy & focus — bonus only (don’t lead; reopens “sports drink”)

6. Master mechanism

UMP — why past solutions failed

She’s been treating a water-holding / barrier-from-within problem as a “buy a richer cream” problem. Forced-air heat + menopause-era drop in skin’s ability to hold water → topicals sit on top. High-sodium sports electrolytes don’t fit desk/winter life.

UMS — how Skin Salt wins

Drinkable skin-support matrix (HA + ceramides + Vit C + DermaVal) carried by everyday electrolytes (500 mg sodium) so water and skin nutrients get in while cream seals outside.

Sodium / no-sweat script

You’re not wrong — you don’t need a sports salt dump when you’re not sweating. Skin Salt is 500 mg sodium — about half of LMNT’s typical 1,000 mg. Winter reason-to-buy is the skin ingredients; electrolytes help water land. BP / sodium limits → talk to your doctor.
